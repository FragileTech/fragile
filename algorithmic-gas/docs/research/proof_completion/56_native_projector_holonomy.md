## 2. Exact parallel projector and its local sector

:::{prf:theorem} Native projector is covariantly parallel and holonomy preserves its line
:label: thm-nph-native-reduction

On EVERY allowed native smooth projector chart, the original connection
is traceless anti-Hermitian and obeys

$$
 \nabla_{\mathcal A}P=dP+[\mathcal A,P]=0,\qquad
 \mathcal F=d\mathcal A+\mathcal A\wedge\mathcal A=dP\wedge dP,
 \qquad [\mathcal F,P]=0 .                           \tag{NPH.5}
$$

For a native path $\gamma$, let its original parallel transport solve
$\dot U=-\mathcal A(\dot\gamma)U,\ U(0)=I$.
Then

$$
 U^\dagger U=I,\qquad \det U=1,\qquad
 P(\gamma(1))=U P(\gamma(0))U^\dagger .               \tag{NPH.6}
$$

Consequently its actual based full and restricted holonomy groups lie in

$$
 H_{P_*}=\{U\in SU(3):UP_*U^\dagger=P_*\}
 \simeq S(U(1)\times U(2))
 \simeq U(2),\qquad \dim H_{P_*}=4 .                 \tag{NPH.7}
$$

They cannot be the unrestricted eight-dimensional $SU(3)$ holonomy.
This is the exact sector of the existing connection, even when the
common-frame projector orbit spans all of $\mathbb{CP}^2$.

On a local native unit section $c$, its induced line connection is
$\alpha=c^\dagger dc$. In an adapted $SU(3)$ frame $(c,E)$ the same
connection is block diagonal, with line block $\alpha$ and complement
block $\mathcal B=E^\dagger(dE+\mathcal A E)$ satisfying

$$
 \operatorname{tr}\mathcal B=-\alpha,\qquad
 c^\dagger\mathcal Fc=d\alpha,\qquad
 \mathcal B=\mathcal B_{\mathfrak{su}(2)}
                              -\tfrac12\alpha I_2 . \tag{NPH.8}
$$

Thus full stabilizer holonomy means the linked native $U(1)$ and
non-Abelian $SU(2)$ sectors; it is the group
$(SU(2)\times U(1))/\mathbb Z_2$, not an extra connection choice.
:::

:::{prf:proof}
Differentiating $P^2=P$ gives $P\,dP\,P=0$ and makes $dP$ off diagonal
relative to $P\oplus(I-P)$. Direct multiplication yields
$[[P,dP],P]=-dP$, proving the first identity.
The commutator of two Hermitian matrices is anti-Hermitian and traceless.
For $X=\partial_aP,Y=\partial_bP$ their off-diagonal blocks give
$[[P,X],[P,Y]]=-[X,Y]$. Differentiating the connection contributes
$2[X,Y]$, so its curvature is exactly $[X,Y]$.
The commutator of two off-diagonal matrices is block diagonal, proving
the last identity in (NPH.5).

The anti-Hermitian/traceless ODE preserves $U^\dagger U$ and its
determinant. Differentiating $U^\dagger P(\gamma)U$ gives zero by
$\nabla P=0$, proving (NPH.6). A loop therefore preserves $P_*$.
In a constant frame with $P_*=E_{11}$ its matrix is
$\operatorname{diag}(z,W)$ with $|z|=1,W\in U(2),z\det W=1$.
This is exactly the connected group in (NPH.7).

For a normalized section, $dP\,c=dc-c\alpha$ and
$\mathcal A c=-dc+c\alpha$. Therefore
$(d+\mathcal A)c=c\alpha$; there are no line/complement cross blocks.
In an adapted determinant-one frame the total trace remains zero,
giving $\operatorname{tr}\mathcal B=-\alpha$.
Taking the line curvature gives $d\alpha$.
Subtracting half its trace identifies (NPH.8).
The displayed stabilizer group decomposition follows from
$(S,e^{i\theta})\mapsto
\operatorname{diag}(e^{-2i\theta},e^{i\theta}S)$,
whose kernel is $\{(I,1),(-I,-1)\}$.
:::

:::{prf:lemma} Ambient frame changes must retain the original comparison connection
:label: lem-nph-native-frame-law

For a smooth unitary frame change $g$, the SAME native connection has
the ordinary frame representation
$\mathcal A^{g}=g\mathcal A g^\dagger-dg\,g^\dagger$ and
$P^{g}=gPg^\dagger$.
Its holonomy and curvature are the conjugate representations of the same
native transport. Recomputing the expression
$[P^{g},dP^{g}]$ with a newly flat ambient derivative instead would give

$$
 [P^{g},dP^{g}]
   =\mathcal A^{g}
      +g\{P\theta P+(I-P)\theta(I-P)\}g^\dagger,
 \qquad \theta=g^\dagger dg .                       \tag{NPH.9}
$$

The additional diagonal term is generally nonzero. An arbitrary local
reframing followed by this recomputation is not silently the original
executed common-frame connection.
:::

:::{prf:proof}
Differentiate $P^{g}$ to get
$dP^{g}=g(dP+[\theta,P])g^\dagger$.
The tangent block identity gives
$[P,[\theta,P]]=-(P\theta(I-P)+(I-P)\theta P)$.
Separating the diagonal and off-diagonal blocks of $\theta$ proves
(NPH.9). The ordinary frame law follows by changing variables in the
original transport ODE, and therefore preserves the same holonomy
by conjugation at its basepoint. No new parallel transport is installed.
:::

## 3. Curvature witnesses give exact native holonomy

:::{prf:lemma} Small native loops turn a primitive curvature witness into full stabilizer holonomy
:label: lem-nph-local-generators

Let $P$ be an existing $C^2$ native descriptor on a connected open chart.
Suppose four actual matrices formed from its curvature, its first
covariant derivatives and their Lie brackets have positive Gram
determinant at a point, and span the four-dimensional stabilizer algebra
$\mathfrak h_{P_*}$. Then the restricted native holonomy is exactly
$H_{P_*}$. It already equals that group on a sufficiently small
neighborhood of the witness point.

In the real native case $P=uu^T$ with its original smooth unit force
$u$, all transport lies in the real $SO(2)$ stabilizer of $u_*$. If
any curvature component is nonzero, its restricted holonomy is that
entire $SO(2)$; if the curvature vanishes identically, its restricted
holonomy is trivial. Possible noncontractible-chart holonomy remains
its original computed loop holonomy.
:::

:::{prf:proof}
We construct actual based loops instead of assuming a holonomy closure
theorem. A signed thin coordinate rectangle at $q$, with one side
$\delta$ and other side parameter $s$, has smooth transport
$h_{q,\delta}(s)$, with $h_{q,\delta}(0)=I$.
Differentiating its path ODE with respect to the thin side gives,
after transporting it to a fixed basepoint,

$$
 \delta^{-1}h_{q,\delta}'(0)\longrightarrow
             -U_{q\leftarrow *}^\dagger
                        \mathcal F_{ab}(q)U_{q\leftarrow *}.
                                                               \tag{NPH.10}
$$

One may verify this directly by multiplying the four side transports:
the first-order terms cancel, and the coefficient of $\delta s$ is
$-(\partial_a\mathcal A_b-\partial_b\mathcal A_a+
[\mathcal A_a,\mathcal A_b])$. The $C^1$ connection gives the same
limit when the fixed side shrinks. All these loops are contractible
inside the original chart.

Move $q$ a distance $\varepsilon$ along direction $c$ using the same
native transport. The derivative of its pulled-back curvature is
exactly $D_c\mathcal F_{ab}$. Subtract the corresponding based
rectangle generator at the basepoint, divide by $\varepsilon$,
and choose $\delta=o(\varepsilon)$.
Products, inverse loops and scalar reparameterization make this
difference an actual tangent of a based holonomy curve; its limit is
the desired covariant derivative.

Similarly, if $h_1,h_2$ are actual based holonomy curves with tangents
$X_1,X_2$, then
$h_1(s)h_2(t)h_1(s)^{-1}h_2(t)^{-1}$ has tangent in $t$ at zero
$\operatorname{Ad}_{h_1(s)}X_2-X_2$.
Dividing by $s$ and shrinking it gives $[X_1,X_2]$.
Iterating gives any of the finite Lie brackets in the witness.

Positive determinant is an open finite-dimensional condition.
Hence sufficiently small but nonzero rectangle, displacement and
commutator parameters produce FOUR actual holonomy-curve tangents
which still form a basis of $\mathfrak h_{P_*}$.
The product of those four curves defines a smooth map
$\mathbb R^4\to H_{P_*}$ with nonsingular derivative at zero.
The ordinary inverse-function theorem puts a neighborhood of identity
inside the actual restricted holonomy subgroup.
By (NPH.7) that subgroup is contained in the connected group $H_{P_*}$.
An open subgroup of a connected group is the whole group, since all
its cosets are open and would otherwise disconnect it. This proves
the assertion without replacing the holonomy by its closure.

For a real native unit section, $\mathcal A$ is real antisymmetric,
$du+\mathcal A u=0$, and its determinant-one transport fixes that
same parallel real vector around loops. It therefore lies in $SO(2)$.
A nonzero curvature component gives a nonzero actual one-dimensional
rectangle tangent and the preceding inverse-function argument in
dimension one gives the whole group.
When $\mathcal F=0$, differentiate the transport along a based
contracting homotopy of a loop. Differentiation of its ODE gives an
integral of transported
$\mathcal F(\partial_s\gamma,\partial_t\gamma)$; the fixed endpoints
give no boundary terms. That integral is zero, so every contractible
loop has identity transport. This proves the stated restricted
classification without discarding noncontractible recorded loops.
:::

## 4. Explicit consensus stabilizer and zero-phase reduction

:::{prf:theorem} The actual nonzero consensus phase has full stabilizer holonomy
:label: thm-nph-consensus-holonomy

$$

  =\begin{cases}
      H_{P_\beta(y_*)}\simeq U(2),&\kappa\ne0,\\
      SO(2)_{n_*},&\kappa=0 .
    \end{cases}                                        \tag{NPH.11}
$$

The zero statement consumes only the zero-admitting phase tag.
An explicit nonzero primitive witness at $x=Re_1$, in its constant
phase-removed frame, is

$$
\begin{split}

                 =-2i\beta(E_{11}-E_{22})/R^2,\\

                 =-2i\beta(E_{11}-E_{33})/R^2,\\

                 =2i\beta(E_{23}+E_{32})/R^4,\\
 \det(\langle X_j,X_k\rangle_F)_{j,k=1}^4

\end{split}                                             \tag{NPH.12}

:::{prf:proof}

$\|X_2\|^2=\|X_3\|^2=8\beta^2/R^4$,

For a chart not containing an axis, use these SAME coordinate curvature

## 6. Actual sector and remaining identification

Every native smooth rank-one projector connection has the exact
parallel-line reduction (NPH.5). In the explicit positive consensus
phase regime its local holonomy is the full four-dimensional stabilizer,
with primitive determinant (NPH.12). Its native semisimple sector is
non-Abelian, while its ambient $SU(3)$ connection remains reducible.
The actual zero phase and proved radial harmonic spatial limit have
the smaller $SO(2)$ sector. A harmonic execution-state chart with
positive original phase has the separate full stabilizer result
(NPH.13); its velocity coordinates have not been identified as physical
space.

As $\kappa\to0$, the determinant certificates vanish with the exact
sixth power of the phase. A family of positive finite-phase holonomy
groups therefore need not preserve that group in its radial limiting
field. This loss is an evaluated native parameter regime, rather than
an assumed uniform nondegeneracy property.

These statements identify the actual connection/curvature sector and
its executed chart/frame law. They retain the original curvature action,
density weight and force-score variations already derived in Chapter
NASI; neither unrestricted gauge-field support nor an unforced
Yang--Mills equation is inserted. A four-dimensional physical local
connection, its physical action/force identification and its continuum
operator estimates still require their separate native spacetime
identification. No missing implementation field or invented gauge
model substitutes for that remaining step.
