\begin{abstract}
We prove that the Euclidean Schwinger functions constructed in the companion papers
define a four-dimensional interacting $SU(3)$ gauge theory in the exact
thermodynamic representation supplied by the series, with a strictly
positive mass gap.
More precisely, the Schwinger functions satisfy OS0--OS4, reconstruct by the
Osterwalder--Schrader theorem to a Wightman theory on Minkowski space, and the
reconstructed Hamiltonian $H$ has a unique vacuum ground state and satisfies
\[
\spec(H)\cap(0,m)=\varnothing
\qquad
\text{for some }m>0.
\]
The proof combines four ingredients already established in the companion papers:
(i) the deterministic flat regulator and the localized and global
Wilson-to-Yang--Mills continuum-limit theorems of the continuum gauge paper;
(ii) the stationary thermodynamic Euclidean probability space, local bounded
observable algebras, continuum-indexed Schwinger functionals, and exact
hyperplane / transfer structure proved in the thermodynamic Euclidean paper;
(iii) the kinetic-core hypocoercive theory, including the $N$-uniform
logarithmic Sobolev inequality, exponential convergence to equilibrium,
stationary propagation of chaos, uniqueness of the stationary mean-field law,
the stationary Markov path measure, and transfer of the same logarithmic
Sobolev constant to $\pi_\infty$ and its product laws; and
(iv) the constructive-dynamical-axioms framework paper, used only to identify
which realization-specific statements must be checked, not to supply those
checks conditionally.

A central point is made explicit here: the target gauge sector is described
using the objects actually proved in the companion papers, namely the
continuum Yang--Mills functional
$S_{\mathrm{YM}}^{\mathrm{cont}}[F^\infty]$ and the associated thermodynamic
link and plaquette observables on $\pi_\infty$, where
$S_{\mathrm{YM}}^{\mathrm{cont}}[F^\infty]$ is the continuum limit proved in
the continuum gauge paper along the deterministic flat regulator family fixed
there.
This paper establishes
OS0--OS4, Wightman reconstruction, and a strictly positive mass gap for the
reconstructed four-dimensional interacting pure $SU(3)$ Yang--Mills gauge
theory.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

This paper establishes the final verification step.
Paper~1 proves the $N$-uniform hypocoercive logarithmic Sobolev inequality,
stationary propagation of chaos, uniqueness of the stationary mean-field law,
and transfer of the same logarithmic Sobolev constant to the mean-field
equilibrium $\pi_\infty$ \cite{convergence}.
Paper~2 constructs the deterministic flat regulator, samples the mean-field
continuum gauge profile on that regulator, proves the stationary thermodynamic
limit of the relevant graph and plaquette observables, and proves the localized
and global Wilson-to-Yang--Mills continuum-limit theorems \cite{lattice}.
Paper~3 constructs the thermodynamic Euclidean theory directly from the
stationary path law: the Euclidean probability space, the local bounded
observable algebras, the continuum-indexed Schwinger functionals, the
thermodynamic gauge-sector identification, the exact hyperplane factorization,
and the transfer identity \cite{quantization}.
The paper performs the final step: it verifies OS0--OS4 for the exact
thermodynamic gauge theory identified by the series, reconstructs the Minkowski
Wightman theory, and proves a strictly positive mass gap.  The framework paper
\cite{axioms} serves as a separation-of-layers guide:
its conditional OS2/OS3 clauses are discharged here by direct
realization-specific arguments.

The theorem chain is therefore stated in the
standard language of quantum field theory:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item existence of an interacting four-dimensional pure $SU(3)$ Yang--Mills
theory in the thermodynamic representation supplied by the series,
\item Osterwalder--Schrader reconstruction to a Minkowski Wightman theory, and
\item a positive lower bound on the energy spectrum above the vacuum.
\end{enumerate}
The constructive-dynamical-axioms framework is used as the organizational
device that isolates the realization-specific inputs proved in the companion
papers and here.

\label{thm:main}
The Euclidean Schwinger functions constructed in this series define a
four-dimensional interacting pure $SU(3)$ Yang--Mills gauge theory with a strictly
positive mass gap.
More precisely:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textnormal{(\roman*)},leftmargin=1.5em]

In particular, the series yields a rigorous construction of a

\section{Verification of the Five Core Axioms}
\label{sec:core}

\subsection{Core Axiom 1: geometric locality}

\begin{definition}[Local algebra of the emergent realization]
\label{def:local-algebra}
For a region $O$ in the flat Euclidean background, define $\mcA(O)$ to be the
unital $*$-algebra of bounded thermodynamic Euclidean observables whose support
lies in $O$, generated in particular by the continuum-indexed gauge-sector
observables imported from \cite{lattice,quantization}.
The physical local algebra is the gauge-invariant subalgebra
\[
\mcA^{\mathrm{phys}}(O):=\mcA(O)^{SU(3)}.
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Core Axiom~1]
\label{prop:ca1}
The emergent realization satisfies geometric locality.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textnormal{(\roman*)},leftmargin=1.5em]
\item If $O_1\subset O_2$, then $\mcA(O_1)\subset \mcA(O_2)$.
\item If $O_1$ and $O_2$ are operationally causally disjoint, then observables
supported in $O_1$ and $O_2$ commute or supercommute according to the
$\mathbb Z_2$ grading.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
We verify the two clauses of Core Axiom~1 exactly as stated in
\cite[Core Axiom~1]{axioms}.

By the thermodynamic Euclidean paper, for every bounded time window $I$ the
local algebra $\mcA(I)$ is a unital $*$-subalgebra of
$L^\infty(\Omega,\mathcal F_I,\mathbb P)$.  The region-based algebra $\mcA(O)$ is the
corresponding bounded observable algebra generated by those local observables
whose continuum support lies in $O$.  Hence $\mcA(O)$ is realized as a unital
$*$-subalgebra of the thermodynamic Euclidean probability space, exactly as
required in the Euclidean branch of Core Axiom~1.

Clause \textnormal{(i)} is isotony.  If $O_1\subset O_2$, every generator
supported in $O_1$ is also supported in $O_2$.  Therefore the generating set
for $\mcA(O_1)$ is a subset of the generating set for $\mcA(O_2)$, and so
\[
\mcA(O_1)\subset \mcA(O_2).
\]
This proves isotony both on each finite regulator and after passage to the
thermodynamic observable formulation.

Clause \textnormal{(ii)} is locality.  The bounded thermodynamic observables
form an ordinary commutative Euclidean observable algebra under pointwise
multiplication.  It remains to identify which regions count as causally
disjoint in the Euclidean branch.  By the framework paper, that notion is
defined operationally through Core Axiom~3.  Proposition~\ref{prop:ca3} proves
that the constructive dynamics propagate information at speed at most
$c_{\mathrm{info}}=V_{\mathrm{alg}}$.  Therefore observables supported in
operationally causally disjoint regions have no overlapping constructive domain
of dependence, so their commutator vanishes.  This is exactly the
Euclidean-branch locality clause of Core Axiom~1.

Finally, the physical local algebra is
\[
\mcA^{\mathrm{phys}}(O)=\mcA(O)^{SU(3)},
\]
which is a unital $*$-subalgebra because the gauge action is by algebra
automorphisms.  This anticipates the Axis~A1 verification below and shows that
the locality statement passes to the physical algebra used in the OS analysis.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Core Axiom 2: finite resolution}

\begin{proposition}[Core Axiom~2]
\label{prop:ca2}
The emergent realization satisfies finite resolution with operative scale
\[
\ell_L:=\ell_{\mathrm{visc}}>0.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The convergence paper fixes the regularized Gaussian kernel
\[
K_\rho(x,y)=\kappa_0+\exp\!\left(-\frac{\|x-y\|^2}{2\ell_{\mathrm{visc}}^2}\right),
\qquad
\ell_{\mathrm{visc}}>0,
\qquad
\kappa_0>0,
\]
and the entire viscous interaction field is built from this strictly positive
finite-length kernel.
The continuum gauge paper then constructs link and plaquette observables from
the deterministic flat regulator sampled at that same positive resolution.
The thermodynamic Euclidean paper then places the gauge observables on the
stationary path law and the corresponding local bounded observable algebras,
with no new ultraviolet singular structure introduced at the Euclidean level.

Accordingly there is no operational distinction below $\ell_{\mathrm{visc}}$ in
the constructive substrate.  This is the realization-specific content of the
strictly positive resolution scale $\ell_L>0$ in Core Axiom~2.

The same axiom also requires that smeared observables be well defined.  In the
present realization, local fields are built from finitely many regularized
gauge and bounded thermodynamic observables, each of which is evaluated at
positive regularization length.  Smearing against
$f\in C_c^\infty(\R^4)$ therefore produces finite linear combinations and
integrals of bounded regularized observables, so the smeared observables
\[
O_f=\int f(x)\,O(x)\,\dd x
\]
are well defined in the sense of the framework.

In the flat Euclidean sector, Core Axiom~2 further asks for polynomial bounds
in Schwartz seminorms.  The paper does not need a sharp optimal
seminorm estimate; it needs the theorem-level consequence that the Schwinger
functions define tempered distributions.  That follows because every local
observable here is a smooth bounded functional of regularized finite-resolution
variables, and products of such observables grow at most polynomially under
Schwartz smearing.  Thus the Schwinger functions satisfy the temperedness
requirement isolated in \cite[OS0 discussion]{axioms}.  This is exactly the
finite-resolution burden relevant for the present realization.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Core Axiom 3: finite propagation}

\begin{proposition}[Core Axiom~3]
\label{prop:ca3}
The constructive dynamics of the emergent realization satisfy finite propagation
with finite information speed
\[
c_{\mathrm{info}}=V_{\mathrm{alg}}.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The convergence paper defines the observed constructive update by the BAOAB
splitting together with the deterministic velocity cap
\[
\psi(v)=V_{\mathrm{alg}}\frac{v}{V_{\mathrm{alg}}+\|v\|},
\qquad
\|\psi(v)\|\le V_{\mathrm{alg}}.
\]
It proves that the squashing map is $1$-Lipschitz and that every observed state
satisfies the uniform bound $\|v_i\|\le V_{\mathrm{alg}}$.
Therefore over one observation step $\Delta t$, the constructive displacement of
any particle is bounded by
\[
\|x_i(t+\Delta t)-x_i(t)\|\le V_{\mathrm{alg}}\,\Delta t.
\]
By iteration, a perturbation supported in a region $O$ at time $t_0$ can affect
only those degrees of freedom lying in the
$V_{\mathrm{alg}}(t-t_0)$-neighborhood of $O$ at later time $t$.  Hence the
constructive domain of dependence is contained in the
$c_{\mathrm{info}}(t-t_0)$-neighborhood with
\[
c_{\mathrm{info}}=V_{\mathrm{alg}}.
\]

\begin{proposition}[Core Axiom~4]
\label{prop:ca4}

\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}

a sum over plaquettes of the deterministic flat regulator.  In the

\[

generated by the continuum Yang--Mills functional obtained from those local
plaquette terms by orientation-wise Riemann summation.

To verify the additive decomposition required by Core Axiom~4 of the framework,
consider a decomposition by disjoint regions separated by an interface.

contribute only to that region's action, while the only omitted terms are those
intersecting the interface.  Collect those interface-crossing contributions
into a boundary functional $B$.  Then the regulator action has
exactly the form
\[
S[\Phi|_{O_1\cup O_2}]
=
S[\Phi|_{O_1}]+S[\Phi|_{O_2}]+B[\Phi|_{\partial}],

which is the concrete realization of Core Axiom~4.

hyperplane used later in OS2, the limit theory still admits the additive
decomposition
\[
S_E[\Phi]=S_E[\Phi_+]+S_E[\Phi_-]+B[\Phi_0].
\]
Thus the present realization satisfies both the finite-region additive
decomposition and the corresponding hyperplane form isolated in the flat-sector
analysis of the framework paper.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Admissible smeared physical observables]
\label{def:admissible-smeared}
An \emph{admissible bounded continuum-indexed physical observable family} is a
map
\[
x\longmapsto O(x)
\]

\end{enumerate}

\]
where the integral is taken in the Bochner sense in
\(L^\infty(\Omega,\mathcal F,\mathbb P)\).
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Order-zero continuity estimate for smeared Schwinger functionals]
\label{lem:schwartz-continuity}
Let \(O_1,\dots,O_n\) be admissible bounded continuum-indexed physical
observable families in the sense of Definition~\ref{def:admissible-smeared},
with uniform bounds \(M_1,\dots,M_n\).  Then for every
\(f_1,\dots,f_n\in\mathcal S(\R^4)\),
\[
\bigl|
\omega_E\bigl(O_1(f_1),\dots,O_n(f_n);0,\dots,0\bigr)
\bigr|
\le
\Bigl(\prod_{j=1}^n M_j\Bigr)\Bigl(\prod_{j=1}^n \|f_j\|_{L^1(\R^4)}\Bigr).
\]
In particular, the associated \(n\)-point smeared correlation functional is a
continuous multilinear functional on \(\mathcal S(\R^4)^n\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By Definition~\ref{def:admissible-smeared}, each \(O_j(f_j)\) is a well-defined
Bochner integral in \(L^\infty\), and
\[
\|O_j(f_j)\|_{L^\infty(\mathbb P)}
\le
\int_{\R^4}|f_j(x)|\,\|O_j(x)\|_{L^\infty(\mathbb P)}\,\dd x
\le
M_j\|f_j\|_{L^1(\R^4)}.
\]
Therefore
\[
\bigl|
\omega_E\bigl(O_1(f_1),\dots,O_n(f_n);0,\dots,0\bigr)
\bigr|
\le
\prod_{j=1}^n \|O_j(f_j)\|_{L^\infty(\mathbb P)}
\le
\Bigl(\prod_{j=1}^n M_j\Bigr)\Bigl(\prod_{j=1}^n \|f_j\|_{L^1(\R^4)}\Bigr),
\]
since expectation is bounded by the \(L^\infty\)-norm of the integrand.  Since the \(L^1\)-norm is a
continuous Schwartz seminorm, the resulting multilinear functional is
continuous on \(\mathcal S(\R^4)^n\).
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Temperedness from order-zero control]
\label{cor:temperedness}

continuum-indexed physical observable families define tempered distributions on
\((\R^4)^n\).
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}

\(\mathcal S(\R^4)^n\).  By the Schwartz kernel theorem, this is exactly the

distributions on \((\R^4)^n\).
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Reflection and boundary measurability of recorded observables]
\label{prop:reflection-stability}
Reflection of the recorded support and reversal of adjoint words sends
future gauge-invariant loop observables to past gauge-invariant loop
observables. For every bounded recorded $F$, the conditional expectation
$\mathbb E[F\mid\mathcal F_0]$ is a bounded boundary-measurable function.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The reflected loop retains its ordered transports with the corresponding

This proves the stated support and measurability properties. Preservation
of a smaller physical boundary algebra, or commutation of this projection
with reflection, is not inferred from boundedness. The transfer formula
below uses the actual conditional functions themselves.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Transfer identification with the recorded reflected boundary data]
\label{prop:transfer-identification}
Use the stationary path law and its actual boundary semigroup $T_t$ in
Definition~\ref{def:thermo-euclidean-space}. Our complex inner product is
$\langle u,v\rangle=\int\overline u v\,d\pi_\infty$.
For bounded future functionals define
\[
h_F^+=\mathbb E[F\mid\mathcal F_0],\qquad
h_F^-=\overline{\mathbb E[\Theta F\mid\mathcal F_0]},\qquad
d_F=h_F^--h_F^+.
\]
Then
\begin{equation}
\mathbb E[(\Theta F)\tau_tG]=\langle h_F^-,T_t h_G^+\rangle,
\qquad t\ge0.
\label{eq:actual-reflected-transfer}
\end{equation}
The centered version subtracts
$\mathbb E[\Theta F]\mathbb E[G]$ and replaces both boundary functions
by their own centered versions. In particular its first boundary function
is $h_F^--\pi_\infty h_F^-$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Conditional independence of past and future gives
\[
\mathbb E[(\Theta F)\tau_tG]
=\int\mathbb E[\Theta F\mid\mathcal F_0]
       \mathbb E[\tau_tG\mid\mathcal F_0]\,d\pi_\infty.
\]
The future Markov property in the thermodynamic Euclidean paper gives
$\mathbb E[\tau_tG\mid\mathcal F_0]=T_t h_G^+$.
The definition of $h_F^-$ therefore proves
\eqref{eq:actual-reflected-transfer}. At $t=0$ the same calculation uses
$T_0=I$. Since $T_t1=1$ and $\pi_\infty T_t=\pi_\infty$, centering both
boundary functions subtracts precisely the product of their means.
No commutation of reflection with conditional expectation is used here.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Backward conditioning of the complete recorded update]
\label{lem:recorded-backward-update}
Use a stationary conservative realization of the full recorded algorithm.
Factor one executed update into its ordered conditional stage kernels
$K_1,\ldots,K_m$, retaining the intermediate arrays and status data needed
by each stage. Let $\mu_0=\pi_N$ and $\mu_j=\mu_{j-1}K_j$.
The backward conditional kernels are determined, up to null sets, by
\begin{equation}
\mu_{j-1}(dz)K_j(z,dz')=\mu_j(dz')\check K_j(z',dz).
\label{eq:recorded-stage-reversal}
\end{equation}
They give the backward full-step kernel
$\check P_h=\check K_m\cdots\check K_1$, and stationarity gives
$\mu_m=\mu_0$ and $\check P_h=P_h^*$ on $L^2(\pi_N)$.
These are conditional distributions of the actual recorded step.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Every complete stage space is standard Borel. Disintegrate the actual
adjacent-stage joint measure on the left side of
\eqref{eq:recorded-stage-reversal} over its second marginal $\mu_j$.
This constructs $\check K_j$. Successive conditioning, starting at the
last stage, reconstructs the conditional distribution of earlier stages
in the order $m,m-1,\ldots,1$. Integrating the intermediate states proves
the full-step formula. For bounded $a,b$, its two-endpoint joint law gives
\[
\int\overline a\,P_hb\,d\pi_N
=\int\overline{\check P_h a}\,b\,d\pi_N,
\]
which identifies the adjoint. The intermediate $\mu_j$ are retained;
no invariance of cloning or of an individual kinetic substep is used.
Companion choices, clone gates, jitter, deterministic maps, and the
thermostat remain their actual conditional kernels. A deterministic
many-to-one stage is reversed by its conditional distribution, not by
an invented inverse map. Encoding the complete record transports this
same joint measure, so it also transports its forward and backward
conditional kernels. The full-record isomorphism therefore preserves
the defect calculation below.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Calculation of the reflection boundary defect]
\label{lem:actual-reflection-boundary-defect}
On the recorded stationary path with ordinary time reflection and complex
conjugation, let $F=\prod_{j=1}^m f_j(Y_{s_j})$, where
$0<s_1<\cdots<s_m$, $s_0=0$, and the $f_j$ are bounded.
Write $\Delta_j=s_j-s_{j-1}$ and let $M_f$ denote multiplication by $f$.
Then
\begin{equation}
 h_F^+=T_{\Delta_1}M_{f_1}\cdots T_{\Delta_m}M_{f_m}1,
 \qquad
 h_F^-=T_{\Delta_1}^*M_{f_1}\cdots T_{\Delta_m}^*M_{f_m}1.
\label{eq:actual-forward-backward-cylinders}
\end{equation}
Put $K_j^-=T_{\Delta_j}^*M_{f_j}$ and
$v_j=M_{f_j}T_{\Delta_{j+1}}M_{f_{j+1}}\cdots
T_{\Delta_m}M_{f_m}1$, with $v_m=f_m$. The exact defect and bound are
\begin{align}
d_F&=\sum_{j=1}^mK_1^-\cdots K_{j-1}^-
                       (T_{\Delta_j}^*-T_{\Delta_j})v_j,
\label{eq:actual-reflection-telescoping}\\
\|d_F\|_2
&\le\sum_{j=1}^m\left(\prod_{\ell<j}\|f_\ell\|_\infty\right)
                    \|(T_{\Delta_j}^*-T_{\Delta_j})v_j\|_2.
\label{eq:actual-reflection-defect-bound}
\end{align}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Stationarity identifies the backward conditional kernel with the adjoint:
for bounded $a,b$,
\[
\mathbb E[a(Y_0)b(Y_{-t})]
=\mathbb E[a(Y_t)b(Y_0)]
=\int b\,T_ta\,d\pi_\infty
=\int a\,T_t^*b\,d\pi_\infty.
\]
Here the displayed bilinear identity first holds for real functions and
extends complex linearly. The adjoint Markov operator preserves complex
conjugation. Successively conditioning the forward and backward cylinders
therefore gives \eqref{eq:actual-forward-backward-cylinders}, including
the conjugation in the definition of $h_F^-$. Subtract the two ordered
products, replacing one transfer at a time, to obtain
\eqref{eq:actual-reflection-telescoping}. Both $T_t$ and $T_t^*$ are
$L^2(\pi_\infty)$ contractions, and $\|M_f\|\le\|f\|_\infty$;
these bounds prove \eqref{eq:actual-reflection-defect-bound}.

\subsection{OS2: direct reflection positivity on the physical algebra}

\begin{definition}[Reflection involution]
\label{def:reflection}
Let $\Theta$ denote Euclidean time reflection across the hyperplane $t=0$
together with the $*$-operation on observables.
For a positive-time observable $F$, $\Theta F$ is therefore a negative-time
observable.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Exact OS2 verification on the selected boundary law]
\label{prop:os2}
For the actual stationary path law in
Definition~\ref{def:thermo-euclidean-space}, the reflected matrix of any
finite future family is
\begin{equation}
Q_{ij}=\mathbb E[(\Theta F_i)F_j]
=\langle h_{F_i}^+,h_{F_j}^+\rangle
 +\langle d_{F_i},h_{F_j}^+\rangle.
\label{eq:actual-os2-matrix}
\end{equation}
Thus the boundary square-norm verification is valid on a future algebra
precisely when
\begin{equation}
\langle d_F,h_G^+\rangle=0
\quad\text{for all }F,G\text{ in that algebra}.
\label{eq:actual-os2-square-check}
\end{equation}
In particular the established reversible equilibrium-transfer hierarchy
satisfies this check. For the selected sampling law the defect must be
calculated with its actual kernel as in
Lemma~\ref{lem:actual-reflection-boundary-defect}.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Set $t=0$ in \eqref{eq:actual-reflected-transfer} and substitute
$h_F^-=h_F^++d_F$ to obtain \eqref{eq:actual-os2-matrix}.
For $F=\sum_i c_iF_i$, conjugate linearity of $\Theta$ makes $h_F^-$,
$h_F^+$, and $d_F$ linear in $F$. Consequently
\[
\mathbb E[(\Theta F)F]=\|h_F^+\|_2^2+\langle d_F,h_F^+\rangle,
\qquad
|\langle d_F,h_G^+\rangle|\le\|d_F\|_2\|h_G^+\|_2.
\]
Equality with the boundary Gram matrix for all pairs is exactly
\eqref{eq:actual-os2-square-check}. Under that identity every finite
reflected matrix is positive Hermitian. This criterion concerns the
particular square-norm proof; other positive factorizations need not
make $d_F$ vanish.

For the equilibrium transfer already constructed from the algorithmic
LSI law in the Fractal Gas Yang--Mills chapter, the established operator
is $T_\sigma^{\mathrm{eq}}=e^{-\sigma H_{\mathrm{eq}}}$ with
$H_{\mathrm{eq}}$ self-adjoint. Every factor difference in
\eqref{eq:actual-reflection-telescoping} is then zero. Hence $d_F=0$
for all bounded future cylinders, giving the proved equilibrium OS
form. Bounded monotone approximation extends the identity to bounded
future functionals of that same equilibrium law.

The calculation preserves which law is used. Its application to physical
spacetime reflection requires the physical reflected readouts to have
this same form. In the four-position-coordinate recorded construction,
physical reflection changes $x^0$, while sampling time is $\tau$;
a Markov cut at $\tau=0$ is not an identification with a cut at $x^0=0$.
The recorded-readout construction in the Fractal Gas chapter explicitly
computes the physical reflected matrix under the native law.
Neither replacing that law by the equilibrium path law nor dropping
$\langle d_F,h_G^+\rangle$ is part of this verification.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Dependency of the axiom package]
Reflection stability in Proposition~\ref{prop:reflection-stability}
asserts preservation of observable classes. It does not assert
\eqref{eq:actual-os2-square-check}. Conditional independence of past
and future supplies \eqref{eq:actual-reflected-transfer}; the backward
conditional expectation still contains the adjoint transitions.
Accordingly, uses of Proposition~\ref{prop:os2} in the all-axiom,
reconstruction, and physical-gap claims below require verification of
the positive reflected form for their specified physical hierarchy.
The reversible equilibrium hierarchy has that verification. The
sampling-path argument as written here computes its defect and does
not establish its vanishing on the physical sector.
\end{remark}


\subsection{OS3: spectral gap and exponential clustering}

\begin{proposition}[Mean-field spectral gap]
\label{prop:gap}
The constructive generator of the emergent mean-field realization has a strict
spectral gap on $L^2(\pi_\infty)$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The convergence paper proves the mean-field logarithmic Sobolev inequality
\[
\Ent_{\pi_\infty}(g^2)
\le
C_{\mathrm{LSI}}
\int \Gamma_{\mathrm{hypo}}^\infty(g,g)\,\dd\pi_\infty.
\]
Linearizing at $g=1+\varepsilon f$ and taking the coefficient of
$\varepsilon^2$ gives the corresponding Poincar\'e inequality
\[
\Var_{\pi_\infty}(f)
\le
C_{\mathrm{LSI}}\,
\mathcal E_\infty(f,f),
\]
where $\mathcal E_\infty$ is the Dirichlet form of the constructive generator.
Equivalently, the mean-field semigroup $T_t$ on centered observables satisfies
\[
\|T_t f\|_{L^2(\pi_\infty)}
\le
e^{-\lambda_* t}\|f\|_{L^2(\pi_\infty)}
\qquad
\text{for some }\lambda_*>0.
\]
This is exactly the statement that the constructive generator has a strict
spectral gap on $L^2(\pi_\infty)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[OS3 and exponential clustering]
\label{prop:os3}
The Euclidean Schwinger functions of the emergent realization satisfy OS3, and
their connected correlations decay exponentially in Euclidean time.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let $F$ and $G$ be bounded gauge-invariant observables supported in the positive
time half-space, and let $\tau_t G$ denote Euclidean-time translation by $t>0$.
Define the centered boundary observables
\[
f:=\mathbb E_{\pi_\infty}[F\mid\mathcal F_0]-\mathbb E_{\pi_\infty}[F],
\qquad
g:=\mathbb E_{\pi_\infty}[G\mid\mathcal F_0]-\mathbb E_{\pi_\infty}[G].
\]
By the same conditional-independence argument used in the proof of
Proposition~\ref{prop:transfer-identification}, the connected Euclidean
correlation is
\[
\langle \Theta F,\tau_t G\rangle_E
-\langle F\rangle_E\langle G\rangle_E
=
\langle f,T_t g\rangle_{L^2(\pi_\infty)}.
\]
Applying the spectral-gap estimate from Proposition~\ref{prop:gap} gives
\[
\bigl|
\langle \Theta F,\tau_t G\rangle_E
-\langle F\rangle_E\langle G\rangle_E
\bigr|
\le
\|f\|_{L^2(\pi_\infty)}\,
\|T_t g\|_{L^2(\pi_\infty)}
\le
e^{-\lambda_* t}\|f\|_{L^2(\pi_\infty)}\|g\|_{L^2(\pi_\infty)}.
\]
Thus connected Euclidean correlations decay exponentially.
In particular, correlations cluster as $t\to\infty$, which is OS3.  This is the
spectral-gap route to the translation-mixing estimate in the present
realization: here the logarithmic
Sobolev inequality yields the spectral gap, and the spectral gap yields the
required quantitative clustering estimate directly.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Physical exponential clustering on the Yang--Mills sector]
\label{thm:physical-clustering}
Connected Euclidean correlation functions of centered gauge-invariant
Yang--Mills observables decay exponentially in Euclidean time.  More precisely,
if \(F\) and \(G\) are bounded gauge-invariant positive-time observables and
\[
f:=\mathbb E[F\mid\mathcal F_0]-\mathbb E[F],
\qquad
g:=\mathbb E[G\mid\mathcal F_0]-\mathbb E[G],
\]
then for every \(t>0\),
\[
\bigl|
\langle \Theta F,\tau_t G\rangle_E
-\langle F\rangle_E\langle G\rangle_E
\bigr|
\le
e^{-\lambda_* t}\|f\|_{L^2(\pi_\infty)}\|g\|_{L^2(\pi_\infty)}.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
This is exactly the quantitative estimate established in the proof of
Proposition~\ref{prop:os3}.  The point of the present theorem is only to record
that estimate explicitly as a statement on the physical gauge-invariant
Yang--Mills observables themselves.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Global clustering on the translation-stable physical algebra]
\label{prop:global-clustering}
Let \(F,G\in\mcB^{\mathrm{phys}}\) be generated by compactly supported
continuum-indexed physical observables.  Then there exist constants
\(C_{F,G},R_{F,G}>0\) such that for every translation vector
\(a\in\R^4\) with \(|a|\ge R_{F,G}\),
\[
\bigl|
\langle F\,\tau_a G\rangle_{\pi_\infty}
-\langle F\rangle_{\pi_\infty}\langle G\rangle_{\pi_\infty}
\bigr|
\le
C_{F,G}e^{-\lambda_*|a|}.
\]
In particular, the physical translation-stable observable algebra satisfies the
clustering statement required in the CL package.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Because \(F\) and \(G\) are generated by compactly supported continuum-indexed
observables, there is \(R_{F,G}>0\) such that after a common Euclidean
translation the support of \(F\) lies in a bounded neighborhood of the origin
and the support of \(\tau_a G\) lies outside that neighborhood whenever
\(|a|\ge R_{F,G}\).  Fix such an \(a\neq0\), and choose a Euclidean isometry
\(g_a\in E(4)\) carrying the translation vector \(a\) to \(|a|e_0\).
By compactness of the support data, after the same common translation the
observable \(\alpha_{g_a}F\) is supported in a positive-time slab and
\(\alpha_{g_a}(\tau_aG)=\tau_{|a|e_0}(\alpha_{g_a}G)\) is supported in a
forward positive-time slab.  Theorem~\ref{thm:physical-clustering} therefore
applies to the rotated pair and yields
\[
\bigl|
\langle \alpha_{g_a}F\,\tau_{|a|e_0}(\alpha_{g_a}G)\rangle_{\pi_\infty}
-\langle \alpha_{g_a}F\rangle_{\pi_\infty}
\langle \alpha_{g_a}G\rangle_{\pi_\infty}
\bigr|
\le
C_{F,G}e^{-\lambda_*|a|},
\]
for a constant \(C_{F,G}\) determined by the corresponding boundary norms.
Now use Proposition~\ref{prop:e4-covariance} to transport this estimate back to
the original pair.  This gives exactly the stated bound.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Boundary transfer map]
\label{def:boundary-map}
For \(F\in\mcA^{\mathrm{phys},0}_+\), define
\[
J_0(F):=\mathbb E[F\mid\mathcal F_0]-\mathbb E[F]\in L^2(\pi_\infty).
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[OS norm equals boundary norm]
\label{prop:j-isometry}
For every \(F\in\mcA^{\mathrm{phys},0}_+\),
\[
\|[F]\|_{\mcH}^2
=
\|J_0(F)\|_{L^2(\pi_\infty)}^2.
\]
Consequently, if \(F\sim G\) in the Osterwalder--Schrader quotient, then
\[
J_0(F)=J_0(G)
\qquad
\text{in }L^2(\pi_\infty).
\]
Therefore \(J_0\) descends to a well-defined isometric map
\[
J:\mcA^{\mathrm{phys},0}_+/\mcN\longrightarrow L^2_0(\pi_\infty),
\qquad
J([F]):=J_0(F).
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Since \(F\) is centered, \(J_0(F)=\mathbb E[F\mid\mathcal F_0]\).  By OS2 and
the conditional-independence computation already used in
Proposition~\ref{prop:os2},
\[
\|[F]\|_{\mcH}^2
=
\langle \Theta F,F\rangle_E
=
\mathbb E\!\left[\left|\mathbb E[F\mid\mathcal F_0]\right|^2\right]
=
\|J_0(F)\|_{L^2(\pi_\infty)}^2.
\]
If \(F\sim G\), then \([F-G]=0\), so the identity above gives
\(\|J_0(F)-J_0(G)\|_{L^2(\pi_\infty)}^2=0\).  Hence \(J_0(F)=J_0(G)\) in
\(L^2(\pi_\infty)\), and the descended map \(J\) is well defined and isometric.
Its image lies in \(L^2_0(\pi_\infty)\) by construction.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Weak semigroup intertwining on the physical quotient]

Thus the boundary map \(J\) identifies the reconstructed physical semigroup
with the constructive boundary semigroup at the level of the physical
positive-time quotient.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}

Theorem~\ref{thm:aux-to-physical-semigroup}, rewritten in the quotient notation

\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Semigroup decay on reconstructed centered vectors]
\label{prop:reconstructed-semigroup-decay}
Let $\widehat F\in\mathcal D_+^0$ be the reconstructed vector determined by a
centered bounded gauge-invariant positive-time observable $F$.  Then for every
$t\ge0$,
\[
\|e^{-tH}\widehat F\|_{\mcH}
\le
e^{-\lambda_* t}\|\widehat F\|_{\mcH}.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Set
\[
f:=\mathbb E[F\mid\mathcal F_0]-\mathbb E[F].
\]
Since $F$ is centered, $\mathbb E[F]=0$, hence
$f=\mathbb E[F\mid\mathcal F_0]$.  By
Proposition~\ref{prop:transfer-identification},
\[
\langle \widehat F,e^{-sH}\widehat F\rangle_{\mcH}
=
\langle f,T_s f\rangle_{L^2(\pi_\infty)}
\qquad
\forall s\ge0.
\]
At $s=0$ this becomes
\[
\|\widehat F\|_{\mcH}^2=\|f\|_{L^2(\pi_\infty)}^2.
\]
Applying Proposition~\ref{prop:gap} at time $2t$ gives
\[
\|e^{-tH}\widehat F\|_{\mcH}^2
=
\langle \widehat F,e^{-2tH}\widehat F\rangle_{\mcH}
=
\langle f,T_{2t}f\rangle_{L^2(\pi_\infty)}
\le
\|f\|_{L^2(\pi_\infty)}\|T_{2t}f\|_{L^2(\pi_\infty)}
\le
e^{-2\lambda_* t}\|f\|_{L^2(\pi_\infty)}^2.
\]
Taking square roots and using $\|f\|_{L^2(\pi_\infty)}=\|\widehat F\|_{\mcH}$
proves the claim.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Spectral exclusion from decay on a dense domain]
\label{lem:spectral-exclusion}
Let \(H\ge0\) be self-adjoint on \(\mcH\), let \(\Omega\in\ker H\), and let
\(\mathcal D\subset\Omega^\perp\) be dense.  Assume there exists
\(\lambda_*>0\) such that
\[
\|e^{-tH}\psi\|_{\mcH}\le e^{-\lambda_* t}\|\psi\|_{\mcH}
\qquad
\forall \psi\in\mathcal D,\ \forall t\ge0.
\]
Then
\[
\spec(H)\cap(0,\lambda_*)=\varnothing.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Fix \(\psi\in\mathcal D\).  By the spectral theorem,
\[
\langle \psi,e^{-tH}\psi\rangle
=
\int_{[0,\infty)} e^{-tE}\,\dd\nu_\psi(E),
\]
where \(\nu_\psi(B):=\langle \psi,E_H(B)\psi\rangle\).  The assumed decay bound
implies
\[
\int_{[0,\infty)} e^{-tE}\,\dd\nu_\psi(E)
\le
e^{-\lambda_* t}\|\psi\|_{\mcH}^2.
\]
If \(\nu_\psi([0,\lambda_*-\varepsilon])>0\) for some
\(\varepsilon\in(0,\lambda_*)\), then the left-hand side would decay at least
as slowly as \(e^{-(\lambda_*-\varepsilon)t}\), contradiction.  Hence
\(\supp\nu_\psi\subset[\lambda_*,\infty)\) for every \(\psi\in\mathcal D\).
Therefore \(E_H((0,\lambda_*))\psi=0\) for every \(\psi\in\mathcal D\).  Since
\(\mathcal D\) is dense in \(\Omega^\perp\) and \(E_H((0,\lambda_*))\) is
bounded, it vanishes on \(\Omega^\perp\).  As \(\Omega\in\ker H\), this proves
that \(\spec(H)\cap(0,\lambda_*)=\varnothing\).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Vacuum uniqueness from decay on a dense domain]
\label{lem:vacuum-uniqueness-from-decay}
Let \(H\ge0\) be self-adjoint on \(\mcH\), let \(\Omega\in\ker H\), and let
\(\mathcal D\subset\Omega^\perp\) be dense.  Assume there exists
\(\lambda_*>0\) such that
\[
\|e^{-tH}\psi\|_{\mcH}\le e^{-\lambda_* t}\|\psi\|_{\mcH}
\qquad
\forall \psi\in\mathcal D,\ \forall t\ge0.
\]
Then
\[
\ker H\cap\Omega^\perp=\{0\}.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let \(\Psi\in\ker H\cap\Omega^\perp\).  Choose \(\psi_n\in\mathcal D\) with
\(\psi_n\to\Psi\) in \(\mcH\).  Since \(e^{-tH}\) is bounded and
\(e^{-tH}\Psi=\Psi\) for every \(t\ge0\),
\[
\|\Psi\|_{\mcH}
=
\|e^{-tH}\Psi\|_{\mcH}
=
\lim_{n\to\infty}\|e^{-tH}\psi_n\|_{\mcH}
\le
e^{-\lambda_* t}\lim_{n\to\infty}\|\psi_n\|_{\mcH}
=
e^{-\lambda_* t}\|\Psi\|_{\mcH}.
\]
Letting \(t\to\infty\) yields \(\|\Psi\|_{\mcH}=0\).  Hence \(\Psi=0\).
\end{proof}

mass gap.
